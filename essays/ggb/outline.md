# Outline — Golden Gate Bridge (4 min, ~600 words)

## Angle

The bridge is credited to the man who promoted it, not the man who designed it.

Strauss sold the bridge for a decade and drew a scheme that was never built. Ellis and
Moisseiff designed the bridge that was. Strauss fired Ellis in 1931, kept his own name on
everything, and the record caught up seventy years late. The argument runs on objects that
still exist and can be photographed: a bronze tablet with a name missing from it, a statue
with a caption, a signature on a drawing.

Ordering principle: start at the moment of maximum physical fact (two towers, no bridge),
then ask whose bridge it is. End on the signature, not on a summation.

Deliberately out of scope for four minutes: the ferries, the bond vote, Giannini, the
labour and the safety net, opening day, the icon. The pool covers all of those and none of
them serve this argument.

## beat: gap
May 1935. Two towers finished, most of a mile of open water between them, an aircraft
flying through the hole where the roadway will go. Establishes the object before the
argument, and buys the right to ask who drew it.

images: construction-29 (3 May 1935, both towers topped out, nothing between them —
                         "the emptiest configuration of the whole build")
        construction-36 (Martin flying boat crossing the gap, both towers bare)
        construction-25 (Dec 1934, warships standing in through the open strait)
        construction-19 (June 1934, both sides of the job in one frame, tide rips visible)
thin:   none — this is the best-served beat in the pool.

## beat: promoter
Joseph Strauss, campaigning since 1921, civic group by civic group. His name is the one
painted on the job.

images: construction-30 / labor-safety-01 (District progress billboard, 1935: "A $35,000,000
                         PROJECT" and "Joseph B. Strauss, Chief Engineer" painted under
                         the span — his name on the object, contemporaneously)
        opposition-money-30 (1937 Fiesta programme chronology: O'Shaughnessy 1917, Strauss's
                         1921 sketch, the Santa Rosa mass meeting)
        people-10 (M. M. O'Shaughnessy, the city engineer who brought Strauss in — one of
                         only two authenticated period portraits in the whole pool)
thin:   **no free photograph of Strauss's face exists anywhere in the pool.** The only
        Strauss face available is the 1941 bronze. Every beat about him has to be carried
        by his name on signage, or by the statue.

## beat: rat-trap
What Strauss actually drew: a cantilever arm from each shore with a suspended span slung
between. Four hundred drawbridges behind him and no suspension bridge. The public called
it the rat-trap.

images: people-08 (the hybrid elevation: braced cantilever arch left, level truss, plain
                         latticed tower carrying a cable — no Deco fluting)
        people-09 (wider crop of the same scheme, approach viaduct included)
        construction-01 (Bonestell dry-point of the bridge as it was actually built —
                         the counter-image to hold against people-08)
thin:   these are 1933 magazine reproductions, not the 1921 sketch itself. Nothing in the
        pool shows the two schemes side by side, which is the one frame this beat wants.
        A hand-drawn two-panel SVG comparison would do more than any photograph here.

## beat: arithmetic
Charles Ellis ran the calculations by hand and wrote the specifications, working with Leon
Moisseiff, whose deflection theory made the span possible. They threw out the hybrid and
moved the Marin pier 200 ft inshore. That is the bridge in every photograph.

images: people-12 (Manhattan Bridge mid-erection, 1909 — Moisseiff's credential, and the
                         exact sequence the Golden Gate would follow: cable geometry
                         finished, deck being hung from it)
        people-16 (Manhattan Bridge today — the shallow stiffening truss on a long flat
                         cable sag, i.e. deflection theory made visible)
        construction-41 (19 May 1936, cables spun, no deck at all — the calculations as
                         built, sharpest file in the pool)
thin:   **severe.** No portrait of Ellis. No portrait of Moisseiff. No calculation sheet,
        no specification page, no drawing from the Golden Gate's own design set. The man
        who designed the bridge is visually absent from a 187-image pool, which is itself
        the argument — but it means this beat runs on somebody else's bridge and on the
        structure. A diagram of the deflection-theory profile, or a typographic card
        quoting the numbers, is the honest fill.

## beat: dismissal
Late 1931. Strauss dismissed him for wasting time and money. One beat, flatly delivered,
no picture needed.

images: people-07 (1933 trade-journal photo of the engineering staff at the finished Marin
                         pier, named in the caption — Clifford E. Paine, the man Strauss
                         promoted to Principal Assistant at roughly the moment he dropped
                         Ellis, and Russell Cone. The replacement, photographed.)
thin:   total otherwise. This is the beat to hold on black or on a typographic slide.

## beat: bronze
1937. The dedication tablet: four columns, twelve names under ENGINEERING STAFF, Strauss
at the head of it and again in the officers' column above. Ellis nowhere on it.

images: people-01 (the tablet itself, every name legible — the omission in bronze; highest
                         resolution copy located)
        people-06 (1937 opening invitation: Rossi, Brown, Filmer. No engineer named at all)
        people-02 / people-03 (the two adjacent dedication tablets: "engineering genius"
                         and "those who, undaunted through the years" — collective,
                         anonymous, nobody named)
thin:   none. This beat is the best-illustrated part of the argument.

## beat: statue
1941. Eight feet of bronze Strauss with a roll of drawings under his arm, paid for by his
widow, captioned "THE MAN WHO BUILT THE BRIDGE".

images: people-17 (statue with the pedestal caption legible — the sharpest artefact of
                         the whole credit problem)
        people-18 (tallest and most detailed read of the face and the rolled drawing)
        people-27 (statue in the lower corner, tower filling the rest of the frame —
                         proportion of credit to object, done by the composition)
        people-26 (heroic low angle against sky, no bridge, no plinth text — presents the
                         myth uncritically and is there to be undercut by the voice)
        people-21 (best 16:9 composition: tiny statue, whole bridge)
thin:   none.

## beat: signature
1949 obituary. 2007 District report. 2012 plaque on the south tower — the same tower that
already carried a 1962 bronze thanking unnamed "courageous, farsighted men" and then
naming fourteen directors. Close on the drawings in the Library of Congress, signed by
Charles Ellis.

images: people-04 (the 1962 tablet on the south tower: anonymous builders, named
                         administrators, on the exact steel that got the Ellis plaque)
        people-23 / people-31 (2012 Legacy Circle plinth: "its FIRST chief engineer" —
                         the District's hedge, in its own lettering, next to the 1941
                         plaque's flat claim)
thin:   **the ending has no picture.** There is no photograph in the pool of the Library
        of Congress drawing sheet, none of the 2012 Ellis recognition plaque on the south
        tower, and no image of Charles Ellis under any licence. The closing line has to
        land on a typographic slide — a set signature block, or the sheet's title block
        rendered by hand. Everything else in this essay ends on a real object; this one
        cannot, and that gap should be designed rather than papered over.

## Pool notes affecting authoring

- `images/` holds 275 files across six prefixes, but `pool/` has only five JSON files.
  There is no `icon-views.json` for the 84 `icon-views-*.jpg` on disk, and `plan.md`
  names nine themes (design, opening-day, modern, tourism) that have neither images nor
  descriptions. Anything after 1937, and anything about the Art Deco design or the colour,
  is effectively unavailable to authoring.
- Several strong images are licensed All Rights Reserved (people-24, people-28, people-29,
  people-30) or non-commercial (people-02, people-03, labor-safety-06/07/12). None of the
  four is load-bearing above; the freely licensed alternatives cover the same beats.
- Aspect ratios are a recurring constraint: much of the best material is portrait
  (people-17, people-18, construction-23) or extreme letterbox (people-16 at 2.26:1,
  labor-safety-05 at 2.6:1).
